Why sump pumps fail

Pumps fail for simple reasons. A float switch jams against the pit wall, a check valve fails and lets water run back, debris clogs the impeller, or the motor wears out. Many failures happen the first time a heavy storm runs the pump for hours.

A power outage during a storm is the other common cause. The storm that floods the cellar also knocks out the grid, so a pump with no backup power stops when it is needed most. A battery or water-powered backup protects against that.

Installing a new pit and pump

A new sump system is a bigger job than swapping a pump.

  1. Locate the low point. The plumber finds where water collects and plans the pit.
  2. Cut and dig the pit. A section of the floor is opened, and a liner is set.
  3. Install pump, check valve and discharge. The discharge line runs outdoors away from the foundation.
  4. Test and restore. The pit is filled and the pump cycles. The floor is patched and the plumber explains maintenance.

Test your pump every season

A five-minute test finds problems before a storm does.

  • Pour a bucket of water into the pit and watch the float rise.
  • Confirm the pump turns on and drains the pit.
  • Check that the discharge line is clear and flows outdoors.
  • Test the battery backup and the alarm.
  • Clear debris from the pit.
