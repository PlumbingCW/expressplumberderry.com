From booking to finished install

The sequence is the same for most homes, whether the heater is the first in a new space or a replacement.

  1. Site visit or phone review. The plumber learns the layout, fuel and current heater.
  2. Written scope and clear price. You see what is included, such as connectors, valves, venting and haul-away, before work starts.
  3. Install day. The old unit is drained and removed. The new one is set, connected, vented and tested.
  4. Walk-through. The plumber shows you the shutoff, the relief valve and the settings, and explains the maintenance schedule.

Venting and combustion air

A gas water heater needs a vent that removes exhaust and enough air for combustion. Houses with tight modern construction or finished basements can starve a heater of air. If your heater sits in a closet, the plumber checks clearances and decides whether louvered doors or another air source are needed.

Changes to the vent can need a permit, and the Derry code enforcement office at 603-432-6148 is the place to confirm. Do not let anyone install a gas unit without checking the vent, and ask the plumber to explain what the check involved.

A note for Derry and nearby towns

Houses built across Derry, Londonderry, Windham, Hampstead and Chester range from old farmhouses to newer homes. Basement layouts, panel sizes and flue types differ widely. Do not assume that your neighbor's install plan fits your cellar.

If you are on a private well, ask the plumber about a sediment filter ahead of the heater, since sediment shortens tank life. The state health department recommends regular well testing, and test results can influence what treatment makes sense.

Questions to ask any installer

These keep the scope honest.

  • Does the quote include a new relief valve, connectors and the discharge pipe?
  • Is a permit needed, and who files it?
  • What happens to the old heater?
  • How is the vent checked and tested?
  • What warranty comes with the unit and with the labor?
