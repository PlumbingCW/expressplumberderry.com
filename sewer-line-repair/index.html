Signs the sewer line itself is damaged

Cleaning fixes blockages and repair fixes pipes. When a line has been cleaned repeatedly and still backs up, the pipe may be cracked, offset, bellied or crushed. A camera shows which, and the plumber measures the distance to the problem so you know where it sits in the yard or under the floor.

Other signs include soft ground or a sunken spot above the line, a sewage smell outdoors, new cracks in a slab and drains that clear and return in weeks. Rodents entering through a broken lateral are another warning sign.

Emergency sewer line repair: when a collapse cannot wait

A fully collapsed line stops all drainage, and sewage may surface in the basement. That is an emergency, and our line is open 24/7 for it. Stop using every fixture and call. While you wait, keep everyone out of the wet area and photograph the damage for your insurer.

The first visit after hours often focuses on making the situation safe. The plumber may clear a temporary path, locate the break with a camera and explain the permanent options in daylight. You get a clear price before any excavation begins.

  • Sewage rising in the lowest drain with a cable that will not pass.
  • A sinkhole or depression above the line.
  • Water in a basement floor drain that does not recede.
  • A line that backs up the same day it was cleaned.

What the camera and locator reveal

Before you approve a repair, you should see why it is needed. The plumber pushes a camera through the cleanout and records the pipe, noting the material, joints and any damage. A locator finds the depth and path above ground, and a spray-paint mark shows it on the lawn.

That information decides everything else. A pipe with one cracked joint at ten feet is a different job than a belly that holds water for twenty feet. See sewer inspection for a plain-language look at what footage can and cannot show.

Older pipes and local conditions around Derry

Derry was settled in 1719 and incorporated in 1827, and the oldest parts of town near the two villages have the oldest sewers. Cast iron and clay lines last many decades, but joints loosen and roots reach them. If you recently bought an older Derry home, a camera inspection before closing can show the condition of the lateral.

Before digging, call 811 so utilities mark their lines. The Derry code enforcement office at 603-432-6148 can tell you whether a permit applies to your repair, and rules vary, so ask rather than assume. We will mention permits when we quote.

Questions to ask before you approve a sewer repair

A repair is a large expense, and these questions keep it honest.

  • Can I see the camera footage of the damage?
  • Is the entire line in good shape apart from this section?
  • What happens to my lawn, driveway or slab?
  • Is there a written scope and price before digging begins?
  • What warranty applies to the repair?
