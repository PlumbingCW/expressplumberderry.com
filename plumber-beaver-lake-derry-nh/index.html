About Beaver Lake

Beaver Lake is a public lake in Derry, once called Beaver Pond. Scottish Presbyterian families settled the area in the early 1700s, and later pavilions, steamboats and public beaches made the lake a place for leisure. The town describes Derry as sitting beside it.

Streets along the water include Beaver Lake Avenue, Beaver Lake Road and North Shore Road, according to property listing sites. We use them as anchor streets and do not claim exact neighborhood limits.

Shoreland rules that can touch plumbing

New Hampshire's Shoreland Water Quality Protection Act covers lakes and ponds larger than 10 acres, so it applies to Beaver Lake. The protected shoreland is the land within 250 feet of the lake's reference line.

For new septic systems, the state sets minimum distances from the lake to the leaching area. They are 125 feet for very porous sand and gravel soils, 100 feet where restrictive layers sit within 18 inches of the surface, and 75 feet for other soils.

A shoreland impact permit is needed for a new system on an undeveloped lot or a replacement that adds sewage loading. Replacing a failed system in kind without added load does not need one. Many towns add stricter rules, so check with Derry as well. Septic design and approval go through the state and town, not through a plumbing call.

Closing a lake house for the winter

A house that sits empty in winter is at risk if a pipe freezes. We do not know how many Beaver Lake homes are seasonal, so use this list only if yours is. It is general New England practice.

  • Close the main shutoff and drain the lines if the house will be empty.
  • Open the faucets so trapped water can drain.
  • Ask a plumber how to handle the water heater and the traps.
  • Know where the shutoff is before you leave.

A frozen or split pipe is an emergency. Close the main valve and call.
