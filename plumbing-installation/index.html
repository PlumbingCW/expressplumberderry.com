Plumbing Installation in Derry, NH

Remodeling a bath, adding a bedroom or swapping fixtures all need plumbing done in the right order. We install new plumbing for Derry homes and shops.

Call (603) 815-9179

Planned installation versus repair

Installation covers anything new or replaced by choice, such as fixtures, a bathroom, a laundry hookup or a second kitchen sink. It happens on a schedule and starts with a plan, which is the opposite of an emergency call. Repairs are fast reactions to a failure, and our faucet repair and toilet repair pages cover those.

A good installation visit starts with a conversation about the layout, the fixtures you chose and the access the plumber needs. The plumber checks the existing supply, drain and vent so the new work fits.

How an installation job runs

The sequence protects the finished room.

  1. Plan and quote. The plumber reviews plans, measures and gives a clear price.
  2. Rough-in. Supply, drain and vent lines are placed before walls close.
  3. Inspection if required. The town may need to inspect the rough-in before drywall.
  4. Finish. Fixtures are set, connected and tested after the finishes are in.

Permits and inspections in Derry

Plumbing installation often needs a permit and an inspection. Derry's code enforcement office, at 603-432-6148, requires 24 hours' notice for inspections. For commercial applications and for new houses or additions with sleeping rooms, an application with the code office and Fire Prevention plan review is needed.

The plumber explains which steps apply to your project. If you are doing part of the work yourself, tell the plumber, because the rules for homeowner work differ from those for contractors, and you should confirm them with the town.

Basements, wells and septic

Basement bathrooms below the sewer line need an ejector pump, and the plumber checks the depth of the existing drain before promising a gravity connection. Houses on private wells need attention to pump capacity when adding fixtures, and houses on septic need to consider the added load on the tank.

Hampstead and Chester have many such homes. Ask the plumber early whether your project fits the system you have. Water heaters come into many remodels too, and the water heater installation page explains that step.

Before you buy fixtures

A few checks prevent returns and delays.

  • Confirm the rough-in dimensions of the fixture.
  • Check that the faucet fits the sink holes.
  • Ask the plumber which brands they can service.
  • Keep the installation manuals.
  • Order early, since backorders slow a project.

Keeping a remodel on schedule

Plumbing sits between demolition and finishes, so a delay in rough-in or inspection holds everyone else. Book the plumber early, confirm the fixture models before the walls close and ask the plumber which dimensions to give the carpenter. A change after the walls are closed costs far more than a change on paper.

Keep a copy of every fixture's installation sheet. They list rough-in heights and drain sizes, and an inspector or a plumber will ask for them.
