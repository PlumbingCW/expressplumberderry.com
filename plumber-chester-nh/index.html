Before We Arrive in Chester

These steps limit damage while a plumber is on the way. They need nothing more than a flashlight.

  • Close the main water shutoff by turning it fully clockwise.
  • Open the lowest faucet in the house to drain the lines.
  • Switch off the water heater if the tank or lines are leaking.
  • Keep people away from wet outlets and the electrical panel.
  • Photograph the damage for your insurer, then call.
  • On a private well, switch off the pump breaker if a pipe is flooding the cellar.

If you smell gas, leave the building and call 911 or the gas utility before you call a plumber.

Wells, septic and what to know in Chester

Most Chester homes depend on private wells and septic systems. The New Hampshire health department recommends testing wells for arsenic, bacteria, chloride, copper, fluoride, hardness, iron, lead, manganese, nitrate and nitrite, pH, radon, sodium and uranium, with volatile organic compounds and PFAS tested at least once. Bacteria and nitrate are checked yearly, and the others every three to five years.

A test tells you whether iron or hardness is wearing out your water heater. If it is, ask the plumber about a filter ahead of the tank. Our water heater repair page explains how scale and sediment shorten tank life.

Radon, arsenic and bacteria are the most common well problems in the state. Those are water-quality issues, not plumbing faults, but a plumber can install the treatment equipment you choose after testing.

Sources: Chester town profile data. NH DHHS and NHDES private well testing brochure.
