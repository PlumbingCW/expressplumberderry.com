What hydro jetting does that a cable does not

Hydro jetting sends high-pressure water through a hose and a nozzle that points backward and forward. The jets cut grease, soften scale and flush debris toward the sewer. A cable punches a hole through the middle of a clog, but the jet cleans the pipe wall all around.

Because a backed-up main line can turn into an emergency overnight, we run jetting calls 24/7. Call the dispatcher, say whether the line has already snaked and failed, and ask whether jetting is likely to help. The answer depends on the pipe, so the plumber confirms it with a camera first.

When a stubborn backup needs jetting right away

If sewage is rising in a basement floor drain after a cable has already failed once, jetting is often the next step. After-hours and weekend calls are normal for this work, because a full main line stops the whole house from using water.

Until the plumber arrives, stop using every fixture and keep people away from the wet floor. Open a window if the smell is strong, and do not use chemical cleaners, since they react with the water and can splash.

How a jetting visit runs

The plumber keeps the job simple and shows you what the line looks like before and after.

  1. Camera first. A small camera confirms the pipe material and finds cracks, offsets or collapsed sections that jetting could make worse.
  2. Choose the nozzle. A penetrating nozzle opens a stoppage. A rotating nozzle scours the walls.
  3. Jet in passes. The plumber works forward and then pulls back, so debris travels toward the sewer instead of settling.
  4. Camera again. A second pass confirms the full diameter is clear, and you see the footage.

Pipes where jetting is the wrong call

Jetting is powerful, so it does not belong in every pipe. Brittle cast iron, cracked clay and any line with an open joint can be damaged by pressure. A camera inspection tells the plumber which pipes are safe, and a good plumber declines to jet a line that the footage shows is fragile.

If the camera shows a break, you move from cleaning to repair. Our sewer line repair page explains spot repair, relining and replacement, and our sewer inspection page explains what the footage reveals.

Jetting in Derry homes and small businesses

Many older Derry homes have cast iron or clay laterals that gather scale for decades. Roots from mature trees on older lots can enter joints and return after each cable pass. A jet clears the root mass and the scale at the same time, though roots grow back unless the joint is repaired.

Restaurants and shops along Broadway and NH 28 face grease more than roots. Their lines respond well to scheduled jetting. If you manage a small commercial building, ask about a maintenance plan on our commercial plumbing page.
