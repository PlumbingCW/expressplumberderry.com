Tankless Water Heater Installation in Derry, NH

A tankless heater heats water as you use it, so there is no tank to run out. Installing one takes more planning than a tank swap.

Call (603) 815-9179

How a tankless water heater works

A tankless heater has no storage tank. When a hot tap opens, flow sensors start a burner or elements that heat the water as it passes through a heat exchanger. You get hot water for as long as the tap runs, within the unit's flow limit, and the unit sits on the wall instead of taking floor space.

That design saves space and removes the standby loss of a tank, but it asks more of the house. A gas unit needs a large gas supply and venting that suits its design. An electric unit may need a heavy circuit that an older panel cannot supply.

Water quality and scale

Mineral scale is the enemy of a tankless unit. The narrow passages of the heat exchanger clog, error codes appear, and flow drops. Annual flushing with a descaling solution is the usual remedy, and the schedule depends on your water.

If you are on a private well in Hampstead or Chester, a water test tells you whether hardness or iron is a risk. The New Hampshire health department recommends testing wells for common contaminants on a regular schedule, and the plumber can suggest a filter or softener based on the results. On town water in Derry, the water department at 603-432-6147 can tell you what it reports about your supply.

The install visit

Tankless installs are more involved than tank swaps, so the plumber walks you through the sequence.

  1. Load calculation. The plumber adds up how many fixtures run at once and confirms the unit's flow rating fits.
  2. Fuel and vent upgrades. Gas lines are sized or replaced, venting is run and electrical circuits are installed as needed.
  3. Mount and connect. The unit goes on the wall with isolation valves so it can be flushed.
  4. Commission. The plumber tests flow, temperature and ignition and shows you the control panel.

Service and flushing

Even a well-installed unit needs service. A flush clears scale, a filter screen cleaning restores flow and an error-code diagnosis finds failed sensors or igniters. If your unit is cold or throws an error, our water heater repair page applies to tankless models too.

Many owners schedule a flush once a year. Others on soft water can go longer. The plumber recommends a schedule after seeing the water and the inside of the unit.

Is a tankless heater right for your house?

These questions help you decide before you call.

  • Do you want to free up floor space?
  • Is your gas line or electrical panel large enough, or are you willing to upgrade?
  • Do you run several showers or appliances at once?
  • Is your water hard or high in iron, and are you prepared to maintain the unit?
  • Are you planning to stay long enough to recover the extra cost of installation?
