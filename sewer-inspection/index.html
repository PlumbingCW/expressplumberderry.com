Sewer Camera Inspection in Derry, NH

A camera shows the inside of your sewer line, so repairs are based on what is actually there. Book it before you buy a house or after a second clog.

Call (603) 815-9179

What a sewer camera inspection shows

A sewer camera inspection puts a waterproof camera on a flexible rod and pushes it through the cleanout toward the street or the septic tank. The plumber watches a screen, records the run and notes the pipe material, joint condition, roots, cracks, offsets and low spots. You receive the findings in plain language.

This is a planned service, and we schedule it when you need information instead of a repair. If the camera finds a problem that needs urgent work, the plumber explains it right away, and our sewer line repair page covers the next steps.

When an inspection is worth booking

Not every house needs a camera. These situations are where the footage saves money.

  • You are buying an older home and want to know the lateral's condition before closing.
  • A drain has been cleared twice in a year and still slows.
  • A cable stopped at a certain distance and could not pass.
  • You see roots in the cleanout or smell sewage outdoors.
  • A previous repair needs to be confirmed with footage.

How the visit runs

The visit is mostly about getting a clear look, and the plumber keeps the process short.

  1. Find access. The plumber locates the cleanout or uses a toilet pull if none exists.
  2. Run the camera. The rod moves in steps while the plumber watches the screen and records distance.
  3. Locate problems. A locator above ground shows the depth and path of any damage.
  4. Review the footage with you. You see the same pictures the plumber sees, and the plumber explains what is normal and what is not.

What the plumber looks for on the footage

Buying or selling a home in Derry

Real estate in Derry spans very old village houses and later construction, and the age of a home hints at its pipe material. Clay and cast iron appear in older lines, while plastic appears in newer ones. An inspection tells you which you have and what shape it is in, without guessing.

If your house is on a septic system instead of a town sewer, the camera is still useful on the line between the house and the tank. Tell the dispatcher which setup you have when you book. After the inspection: your options

You get a clear price before any repair. If the footage is clean, you pay for the inspection and move on. If it shows a small issue, you can monitor it, and the plumber suggests a timeline. If it shows a failing pipe, the plumber explains repair, lining and replacement, and you can compare quotes using the footage.

Many owners pair an inspection with sewer line cleaning so the camera sees the pipe wall instead of grease. The plumber advises whether that helps your case.
