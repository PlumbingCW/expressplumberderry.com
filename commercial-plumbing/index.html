When the business cannot open

A sewer backup in a restaurant, a burst line in a shop or a failed water heater in a salon can stop work immediately. Shut the water to the affected area, stop using fixtures that drain to the blocked line and call the 24/7 line. Tell the dispatcher what kind of business it is and what is not working.

Commercial buildings often have multiple tenants and shared lines. Say whether the problem affects one unit or the whole building, and whether you own the building or lease it. The answer shapes who must approve the work and who pays for it.

  • Sewage backing up in a restaurant kitchen or restroom.
  • A broken supply line flooding retail or office space.
  • No hot water for a kitchen, salon or clinic.
  • A grease trap that is full or overflowing.
  • A floor drain backing up during business hours.

What commercial plumbing differs from residential

Commercial systems are bigger, more complex and more heavily used. Pipe sizes are larger, fixtures serve many people, and the building may have backflow preventers, grease interceptors, floor sinks and flushometer toilets. A repair that takes an hour in a house can take a day in a commercial space.

Rules also differ. Businesses face inspections that homes do not, and some work requires permits and backflow testing. The Derry code enforcement office at 603-432-6148 explains that commercial applications need a code office application and, in some cases, Fire Prevention plan review.
