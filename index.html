When a plumbing problem cannot wait

An emergency plumber in Derry earns the name when water is moving where it should not, sewage is coming back up a drain, or the house has lost heat or hot water in a cold week. Those jobs do not wait for business hours, and our line is open 24/7 for exactly that reason.

While you wait for the plumber, shut off the water at the main valve and keep everyone away from wet electrical outlets. Then call and tell the dispatcher what you can see. A clear description lets the right tools ride in the truck the first time.

  • Water running from a burst or split pipe: close the main shutoff, then call.
  • Sewage backing up in a tub, floor drain or basement: stop using every drain and call.
  • No hot water, or a water heater leaking at the base: switch off the heater and call.
  • A sump pump that stopped while the basement is taking on water: call right away.
  • A toilet that overflows and will not stop: close the valve behind it, then call.

What happens when you call

The call is short on purpose, because a person with water on the floor needs action more than a script.

  1. A dispatcher picks up. You describe the problem, give the address and say whether water is still running.
  2. You get safe next steps. That usually means which valve to close and which room to stay out of.
  3. A plumber is assigned. You hear what the dispatcher can promise about timing, and we never guarantee an arrival window that traffic or weather could break.
  4. The price comes before the work. The plumber diagnoses the problem, explains the repair options and gives you a clear price, and nothing starts until you agree.

Plumbing in Derry homes: what the town's history means for your pipes

Derry was settled in 1719 and incorporated as its own town in 1827. The state's community profile counts 13,776 housing units in Derry (American Community Survey, 2014 to 2018), from farmhouses near the villages of Derry and East Derry to homes built in later decades. That mix matters, because an older house can still carry galvanized steel supply lines that corrode from the inside and lose pressure over time.

The Derry Water Department publishes a lead service line inventory that shows how many lines it could classify. Of 4,466 service lines, none were classified as lead, 20 were galvanized lines requiring replacement, and 2,144 were still marked unknown. If your line is unknown, the town offers a free lead test, and its water office is at 603-432-6147.

Before the plumber arrives: a short checklist

These steps limit damage and give the plumber a head start. None of them needs tools beyond a flashlight.

  • Find the main water shutoff, usually where the line enters the cellar or utility room, and turn it fully clockwise.
  • Open the lowest faucet in the house to drain the lines and take pressure off a leak.
  • Switch off the water heater breaker or gas control if the tank is leaking or the lines are open.
  • Move rugs, boxes and electronics out of the wet area, and photograph the damage for your insurer.
  • Keep a bucket under active drips and note which fixtures still work.
  • Open the door, put pets away and clear a path to the cellar or crawlspace.

If you smell gas, leave the building and call 911 or the gas utility before you call a plumber.

How to check any plumber's license in New Hampshire

New Hampshire licenses master and journeyman plumbers through the Mechanical Licensing Board, which sits under the Office of Professional Licensure and Certification. Practicing plumbing without a valid license is addressed in RSA 153:37. Before any plumber opens a wall in your house, ask for their license information and verify it through the state licensing office.

Whoever comes to your door, you can ask to see license information, and a good plumber will not mind.
