How to pick the right page

Plumbing problems rarely announce their cause. A wet ceiling can come from a supply line, a drain, a tub overflow or a roof leak, so start with the symptom and let the plumber confirm the source. The pages above describe symptoms, local causes and repair options in plain terms.

If water is moving right now, skip the reading and use the checklist on the burst pipe repair page, then call. If the problem is a slow drain or a drip, the drain cleaning and faucet repair pages explain what a visit involves.

Planned work and emergency work are priced differently

A scheduled water heater swap is easier to quote than a midnight leak, because the plumber can plan access, parts and permits. For planned jobs, read the water heater installation and plumbing installation pages first. They explain what to decide before you call.

For both kinds of work you get a clear price before work starts. Ask the dispatcher whether anything about the time of your call changes the number.

What to have ready when you call

A clear description speeds up every plumbing visit. Note what you see, hear and smell, when it started and whether water is still moving. Know where the main shutoff is, and know the fuel and age of your water heater if the problem involves hot water. Have your address and any gate or door codes ready. If you are unsure which service you need, call (603) 815-9179 and the dispatcher will help you sort it out.

If you have photos, keep them for the plumber and for your insurer. A clear picture of the leak, the water heater label or the pipe under the sink can answer questions that take minutes to describe on the phone. Photos also help when you describe a leak to an insurer later. Planned work is easier to schedule when you call during the day, and the line stays open for emergencies at night.
