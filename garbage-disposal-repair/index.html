Leaking from below the sink

If water is leaking from the disposal body or from the pipes under the sink, run no water and switch the disposal off at the wall switch. Empty the cabinet, put a bucket under the leak and call the 24/7 line. A leak from the disposal housing means the unit has failed and needs replacement.

A disposal that hums but does not spin is jammed, and many jams are cleared with a hex key turned in the bottom socket. If you try it, switch off the power first and never put your hand in the unit. If the unit trips the breaker, stop and call.

  • Water pooling in the cabinet under the sink.
  • A disposal that hums but does not spin.
  • A reset button that trips repeatedly.
  • A sink that backs up when the disposal runs.
  • A burning smell or smoke from the unit.

What not to put down a disposal

A disposal is not a garbage can. Grease coats pipes, fibrous foods wrap around the impeller, and starchy foods form a paste that clogs the drain. Bones, coffee grounds in large amounts, pasta, rice and potato peels are frequent culprits.

Run cold water while the disposal operates and for some seconds afterward. Grind small amounts at a time. If the kitchen drain backs up often, the drain line may need clearing, as covered on our drain cleaning page.

Repair, replace or remove

A jam or a loose connection is a repair, and a cracked housing or a burned-out motor is a replacement. When a disposal is more than a decade old, replacement often costs less than repeated repair. The plumber also checks the wiring, the dishwasher connection and the drain trap during the visit.

Some households choose not to have a disposal at all. The plumber can remove one and cap the connection, but septic systems have their own considerations. Homes in Hampstead and Chester on septic should ask the plumber and their septic contractor before relying on a disposal.

Installing a new disposal

A new install needs a sink with the right opening, a nearby outlet or hardwired connection and a drain trap that fits. The plumber mounts the flange, connects the dishwasher drain and wiring if needed and tests for leaks. Sizing depends on how much cooking you do and the strength of the motor you want.

If you are remodeling the kitchen, mention it. Our plumbing installation page covers the broader planned work, and the plumber can combine visits.
