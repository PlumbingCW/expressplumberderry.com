What a replacement visit includes

A swap is more than lifting one tank out and another in. These steps are the ones that protect your house.

  1. Shut off and drain. The plumber closes water and power or gas, then drains the old tank to a floor drain or outdoors.
  2. Disconnect and remove. Supply lines, vent connections and the relief valve discharge pipe come off, and the old tank leaves with the plumber.
  3. Set and connect the new unit. New connectors, a new relief valve and a proper discharge line are installed. Old valves that stick are replaced.
  4. Fill, test and start. The plumber fills the tank before power goes on, checks for leaks, tests the venting on gas units and explains the settings.

Choosing the type of replacement

You do not have to choose the same type of heater you had. These are the usual options and their trade-offs.

TypeStrengthConsider
Standard electric tankSimple, no ventingHigher running cost where electricity is expensive
Gas tankFast recoveryNeeds a safe vent and gas supply
Heat pump (hybrid) tankUses much less electricityNeeds space, airflow and a drain for condensate
TanklessEndless hot water, small footprintHigher installed cost, may need gas line or electrical upgrade

Sizing a heater for your household

A tank that is too small runs out of hot water on busy mornings, and one that is too large wastes energy keeping water hot. The plumber considers the number of bathrooms, the people in the house and the peak demand. Sizing rules are on the heater's own literature, and the plumber reads them with you.

If you are replacing an old heater because it failed on a weekend, the dispatcher can still install a like-for-like unit that day and talk about an upgrade later. Our water heater installation page covers the planned route, and our tankless water heater page covers that upgrade.

Permits, venting and local rules

Some water heater work needs a permit from the town. Derry's code enforcement office can tell you what applies at 603-432-6148, and the plumber will mention it when you book. Do not assume that a quick swap skips this step, because gas and venting changes are the ones inspectors care about most.

What to decide before the plumber arrives

A short list helps the visit go fast, even at night.

  • Where the heater sits, and a clear path from the door to it.
  • The fuel type and the current size.
  • Whether you want a like-for-like swap today or a different type later.
  • Where the old unit can go if you want to keep any part.
  • Who will be home to sign off on the work.
