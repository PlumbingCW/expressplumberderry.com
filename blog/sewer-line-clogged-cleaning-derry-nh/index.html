Clogged Sewer Line Cleaning in Derry, NH: Your Options

When every drain in the house is slow, the main sewer line is the suspect. This guide compares the ways it gets cleared.

By the Derry Express Plumbing team. Published November 2, 2026. Updated November 2, 2026.

Call (603) 815-9179

The short answer: cable first, jet if the cable fails, camera if it returns

A cable machine with a cutting head punches through most clogs in minutes. If the blockage is grease or scale, the cable may open only a small channel, and a hydro jet scours the pipe wall. If the clog returns, a camera shows why, and repair becomes the next question.

Which method fits depends on the pipe, the cause and the history. The plumber chooses based on your description and a few tests, and explains the plan before starting.

What to do while you wait

Stop using every fixture. Each flush and each load of laundry adds water to a line that cannot drain. Keep children and pets out of any wet area, and open windows if the smell is strong.

If water is coming up in a basement floor drain, move belongings off the floor and photograph the damage for your insurer before you clean up. Call the 24/7 line and describe where the water is rising.

  • Do not pour chemical cleaners down the drain.
  • Do not run the washer or dishwasher.
  • Keep the area around the cleanout clear.
  • Note when the problem started and which fixtures are affected.

Why the clog formed

Grease cools and sticks to pipe walls, wipes do not break down, tree roots enter at joints and old pipe sheds scale. A cleaning treats the symptom. Knowing the cause tells you whether to change habits, schedule maintenance or repair the pipe.

Our sewer line cleaning page describes the service, and our hydro jetting page explains when a jet is better than a cable.

What the visit looks like

A good plumber shows you the result.

  1. Find the cleanout. The plumber opens the cap and checks for standing water.
  2. Run the machine. A cutting head or a jet works toward the blockage.
  3. Test the flow. Water runs through several fixtures to confirm the line is clear.
  4. Review and recommend. If debris looks like roots or scale, the plumber suggests a camera.

Why you should not keep flushing

When the main line is partly blocked, water drains slowly but still drains. It is tempting to keep using the house and hope it clears. Each flush and each load of laundry adds water that the line cannot handle, and the slow drain becomes a backup into the lowest fixture.

Sewage in a basement is a health hazard and a cleanup bill. Stopping water use the moment you notice several slow drains keeps the line from filling, and the plumber then arrives to a manageable problem instead of a flooded floor.

What the plumber needs from you

Tell the plumber where the cleanout is, if you know. Clear the area around it, and move stored items away from the floor drain. Mention the age of the house, any past root problems and whether previous plumbers used a cable or a jet. That history helps pick the machine.

Let the plumber know if you are on a septic system. A full tank behaves like a main line blockage, but cleaning the line will not help. If the tank is the issue, the plumber says so and points you toward a septic contractor, which saves time and money.

Preventing the next clog

Most main line clogs have a pattern. Grease, wipes, and roots account for a large share, and each has its own prevention. Collect cooking fat in a can, throw wipes in the trash even if the package says flushable, and ask about root control if trees grow near the line.

A cleanout is a useful addition if the house does not have one. It gives the plumber direct access to the line and makes future cleaning cheaper. Ask about it during the visit.

If the camera shows scale or roots, schedule maintenance cleaning at the interval the plumber suggests. A planned visit on a quiet morning costs less than a backup on a holiday weekend.

What a good invoice shows

After the visit, you should receive a note that says what method was used, how far the machine went and what came out. If the plumber used a camera, ask for the footage or a few still images. This record helps if the clog returns, and it helps the next plumber choose a method.

Keep it with your house records so you can see patterns over the years.

Cleaning around the cleanout

Wear gloves when you handle the cleanout cap, and keep children and pets back. A cap under pressure can release wastewater when it is loosened, so let the plumber open it. Afterward, wash the floor with a household disinfectant and let the area dry fully before storing items there again.

Derry notes on sewer service

Not every Derry street is on town sewer. A house on a septic system has a tank and a leach field instead, and a full tank looks like a clogged line. The plumber checks both, and a septic contractor handles the tank.

The Derry code enforcement office at 603-432-6148 can say whether repairs to the sewer lateral need a permit. Call 811 before any digging so utilities are marked.
