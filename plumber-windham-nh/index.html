Before We Arrive in Windham

These steps limit damage while a plumber is on the way. They need nothing more than a flashlight.

  • Close the main water shutoff by turning it fully clockwise.
  • Open the lowest faucet in the house to drain the lines.
  • Switch off the water heater if the tank or lines are leaking.
  • Keep people away from wet outlets and the electrical panel.
  • Photograph the damage for your insurer, then call.
  • If you are on a well, switch off the pump breaker when a pipe is flooding the cellar.

If you smell gas, leave the building and call 911 or the gas utility before you call a plumber.

What makes Windham plumbing different

Windham has about 15,817 residents across 27.8 square miles and was incorporated in 1742. Its main roads are NH 111, NH 28, NH 111-A and NH 128, and I-93 Exit 3 (NH 111) connects the town to the highway. A plumber coming from Derry typically takes NH 111 or NH 28. Windham High School opened in 2009, and newer construction mixes with older farmhouses across town.

That mix matters for plumbing. Newer houses tend to have plastic supply lines and modern venting, while older homes may still carry copper or galvanized lines that have been repaired many times. Ask the plumber to identify what you have, and see our leak detection page if your water bill has gone up without a reason.

Water, ponds and what they mean for a cellar

Cobbetts Pond has the town beach, and Canobie Lake forms part of the eastern border. Houses near ponds often have higher groundwater, so cellars near water may take on seepage in spring. A working sump pump with a clear discharge line is the first line of defense, and the basement waterproofing page explains what else can help.

The Windham Rail Trail runs 4.1 miles and extends into Derry. Homes along the trail corridor sit at a range of elevations, so one house may stay dry while its neighbor floods.

Many Windham homes rely on private wells and septic systems. The New Hampshire health department recommends testing a private well for arsenic, bacteria, nitrate, radon and other common contaminants on a regular schedule. A test result helps decide whether a filter or softener is worth adding ahead of your water heater.
