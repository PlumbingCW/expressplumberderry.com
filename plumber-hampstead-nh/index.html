Hampstead plumbing starts with the property, not the street

Hampstead has about 8,998 residents across 14.1 square miles, and it was incorporated in 1749 as Timberlane Parish. Its main routes are NH 111, NH 121 and NH 121A. A plumber from Derry usually uses NH 111 or NH 121. Pinkerton Academy serves as the public high school by tuition, so many families drive between the two towns daily.

Many properties here run on a private well and a septic system. That changes which problems are likely and who fixes them. A pressure tank, a well pump and a leach field are outside house plumbing, but the pipes inside the house are ours, and the plumber is clear about where the line falls.

Before We Arrive in Hampstead

These steps limit damage while a plumber is on the way. They need nothing more than a flashlight.

  • Close the main water shutoff by turning it fully clockwise.
  • Open the lowest faucet in the house to drain the lines.
  • Switch off the water heater if the tank or lines are leaking.
  • Keep people away from wet outlets and the electrical panel.
  • Photograph the damage for your insurer, then call.
  • If your house is on a well, switch off the well pump breaker.

If you smell gas, leave the building and call 911 or the gas utility before you call a plumber.

Ponds, meetinghouses and groundwater

Island Pond lies in the southwest, and Wash Pond (also called Sunset Lake) and Angle Pond are other water bodies. Cellars near the water often sit closer to the water table, so seepage and sump pump demand are higher. The sump pump page explains pits, pumps and backup power.

The Hampstead Meetinghouse dates from about 1749 to 1768 and has been on the National Register of Historic Places since 1980. Many surrounding homes are also old, and older houses can carry older supply lines. If yours does, our repiping page explains when replacement beats repeated repair.

The state health department recommends private well owners test regularly, with bacteria and nitrate checked yearly and other items every three to five years. PFAS are found at elevated levels more often in southern New Hampshire, so ask your lab about them. Test results shape whether filtration makes sense.
