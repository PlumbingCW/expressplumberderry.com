The toilet problems we see most

Toilet calls fall into a few groups. Clogs top the list, followed by leaks at the base or tank, tanks that fill slowly or run constantly, and bowls that rock on the floor. Each has a different cause, and the repair ranges from a five-minute adjustment to a full replacement.

A toilet that runs on its own is usually a worn flapper or a fill valve, and a repair is often inexpensive. A toilet that leaks at the base usually needs a new wax ring or a repaired flange, and the plumber checks the floor for rot. A toilet that clogs every week points at a drain issue or an old low-flow model.

Repair or replace?

A toilet is worth repairing when the porcelain is sound and the fix is a part. Replacement makes more sense when the bowl is cracked, the toilet is very old and uses far more water per flush than current models, or repairs have come twice in a year. The plumber explains both costs and lets you choose.

If you are putting in a new toilet, the details of the flange matter as much as the bowl. A flange set too low or cracked is a common reason for a toilet that rocks, and our plumbing installation page explains what a proper setting involves. A toilet on a floor with water damage may need subfloor work too.

What the plumber checks beyond the toilet

A toilet is the lowest point of the bathroom drain system, so it often shows the first sign of a larger problem. If a second fixture gurgles, or the water in the bowl rises and falls when the washer drains, the plumber checks the vent and the line. Sometimes the answer is a drain cleaning visit instead of a toilet repair.

These checks take two minutes and give the dispatcher useful detail.
