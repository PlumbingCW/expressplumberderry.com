Salem is a busy town with a lot of buildings

Salem has about 30,089 residents across 25.9 square miles and was incorporated in 1750. It is the first New Hampshire town northbound on I-93, and NH 28, NH 38, NH 97 and NH 111 cross it. Plumbers usually reach Salem on NH 28 or NH 111 from Derry, and drive time varies with traffic on those roads, so the dispatcher gives an estimate by address.

Salem combines big retail with older neighborhoods and many apartments, offices and shops. The Mall at Rockingham Park and Tuscan Village, built on the former Rockingham Park racetrack with construction beginning in 2017, are major draws. Each kind of building has its own plumbing profile.

Before We Arrive in Salem

These steps limit damage while a plumber is on the way. They need nothing more than a flashlight.

  • Close the main water shutoff by turning it fully clockwise.
  • Open the lowest faucet in the house to drain the lines.
  • Switch off the water heater if the tank or lines are leaking.
  • Keep people away from wet outlets and the electrical panel.
  • Photograph the damage for your insurer, then call.

If you smell gas, leave the building and call 911 or the gas utility before you call a plumber.

Places that shape Salem's plumbing

Canobie Lake Park opened in 1902 and the Icenter, Hedgehog Pond Park and Gordons Hill (366 feet) are other well-known spots. America's Stonehenge is also in town. The Spicket River, Policy Brook, Arlington Mill Reservoir and World End Pond are among the local waterways, and homes close to them may face higher groundwater in spring.

A cellar near a stream or pond is more likely to need a sump pump. Our basement waterproofing page explains how to find the cause before buying a system.

Older Salem neighborhoods may have original sewer laterals made of clay or cast iron. Roots and age cause repeat clogs, and a camera shows why. The sewer line repair page explains options after the footage.
